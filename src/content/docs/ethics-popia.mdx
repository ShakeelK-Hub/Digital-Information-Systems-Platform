## Why this matters

A retail business may collect names, phone numbers, delivery addresses, purchase histories and payment-related records. These details help the business process orders and serve customers, but they must be handled responsibly.

Poor information handling can expose customers to harm, damage trust and create legal or financial consequences for the business. Privacy and security should therefore be considered when choosing, configuring and using information systems.

:::tip[Key takeaway]
Collect only the information the business genuinely needs, use it for an appropriate purpose, restrict access and protect it throughout its lifecycle.
:::

## Key concepts

### Data ethics and privacy

**Data ethics** concerns whether collecting and using information is fair, responsible and appropriate. **Privacy** concerns how personal information is handled and protected. A business should consider not only whether it can collect information, but also whether it should collect it and how it will affect the people concerned.

### POPIA

South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 establishes conditions for the lawful processing of personal information. Its principles address accountability, processing limitations, purpose specification, information quality, openness, security safeguards and the participation rights of data subjects. [[6]](/sources/#ref-6)

The exact obligations depend on the circumstances. A checklist can help a business identify controls, but it does not by itself guarantee compliance. Businesses should consult the Act and relevant Information Regulator guidance. [[7]](/sources/#ref-7)

### Common retail privacy risks

- **Unnecessary collection:** asking customers for information that is not needed for the transaction.
- **Purpose misuse:** using a delivery address collected for an order for an unrelated purpose without an appropriate legal basis.
- **Unauthorised access:** staff or outsiders accessing customer records without permission.
- **Weak security:** using shared accounts, weak passwords or unprotected devices.
- **Improper disclosure:** sharing customer information with a third party without properly assessing the purpose and applicable requirements.
- **Poor retention and disposal:** keeping personal information indefinitely or disposing of records insecurely.

## Example from a retail business

A clothing shop collects a customer's name, phone number and delivery address to deliver an online order. An employee later exports customer details to a personal spreadsheet to create a marketing list.

The original collection for delivery does not automatically justify every later use. The shop should assess the proposed use, its lawful basis and its privacy implications before proceeding. Access should be restricted, and customer information should not be copied into unapproved tools.

## Practical safeguards

1. **Explain collection and use.** Provide an appropriate privacy notice that tells customers what information is collected, why it is needed and how it is handled.
2. **Minimise collection.** Request only information that is necessary for the stated purpose.
3. **Control access.** Give staff individual accounts and permissions appropriate to their roles. Use strong passwords and multi-factor authentication where available.
4. **Protect systems.** Apply software updates, maintain suitable backups and protect devices containing business information.
5. **Train staff.** Explain how to handle personal information, recognise suspicious activity and report incidents promptly.
6. **Review providers.** Assess the privacy and security practices of delivery, payment, e-commerce and cloud service providers.
7. **Set retention and disposal rules.** Keep records for appropriate periods, taking applicable legal and business requirements into account, and dispose of them securely when no longer required.
8. **Prepare for incidents.** Establish a response process and determine what notifications or other actions are required if personal information is compromised.

SAICA also provides POPIA-related resources that can support further research. [[8]](/sources/#ref-8)

## Benefits and trade-offs

Good privacy practices can strengthen customer trust, improve record management and reduce exposure to avoidable incidents. However, security measures require time, staff training and ongoing maintenance. Controls should be proportionate to the type and sensitivity of information being handled.

Privacy and security should be considered before selecting a system, not added only after a problem occurs.

## Applying this to the SME scenario

For a small retailer introducing online orders or a loyalty programme, start by mapping the personal information collected at each step. Identify the purpose for each data item, who needs access, which providers receive it and how long it should be retained. Use this assessment to select suitable system settings and safeguards.
